Image-restoration comparisons often omit the arm that does nothing. We report
three evidence-bounded comparisons from one project that retained that arm,
plus two predeclared follow-ups. At toy scale, two matched-budget networks both
finish below the degraded input: \ToyConv{} and \ToyTrans{} dB versus
\ToyDegraded{} dB. Giving the better arm a \BudgetFold{} budget lowers its
training loss \ToyLossDrop{} times but moves fidelity \ToyLongRegression{} dB
the wrong way. The transformer's stall, attributed by the project to
zero-initialised gates, reproduces in \InitDefaultStall{}/\InitSeeds{} seeds
under the default initialisation and vanishes in \InitZeroEscape{}/\InitSeeds{}
under the scheme that zeroes the gates exactly; the attribution is withdrawn.

At subset scale, a released diffusion model completes on \SubsetN{} crops of
the published RealSR crop128 protocol and loses to bicubic on PSNR on
\LossesBicubic{}, by \GapBicubicAbs{} dB. On a learned perceptual distance
scored on the same crops the order reverses: the model is closer on
\LpipsWins{} of \SubsetN{}, sign test \LpipsSignP{}. A separate
hardware-selected census (\CensusTotal{} inputs, \CensusSkipped{} skipped) is
not the crop128 comparison.

The recommendation: print the trivial arm beside the method, and when it wins
on one endpoint, print the other before publishing the ranking.
